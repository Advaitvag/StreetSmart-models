# Design 1: Final Hardware Design and Bill of Materials

Design 1 is a dedicated unit in each vehicle with its own camera, computer, GPS, Wi-Fi and cellular. It finds potholes during the shift and tags each one with GPS. The advisor review on 2026-10-30 picked Design 1 ([Technical Specification §7.1](SeniorDesign/Technical_Specification.md)). This page is the final version of that design, with the parts needed to build one unit.

Everything is in **one housing mounted behind the rear-view mirror**, like a dashcam: computer, camera, GPS, modem and antennas. A single thin cable runs along the headliner to a small relay box at the fuse box.

---

## 1. Final Design 1
### 1.1 How the unit works

1. **Power.** The unit takes power from the vehicle battery, 12 V in cars or 24 V in trucks. When the ignition comes on, a delay relay at the fuse box turns on 12 V to the unit. A converter inside the unit turns that into 5 V for the board. When the ignition goes off, the unit sees it on the ignition-sense wire and stays on for up to 30 minutes (adjustable) to finish uploading, then shuts itself down. If it has not shut down when the timer runs out, the relay cuts power. See §1.3 for the wiring.
2. **Capture.** The USB camera sits inside the housing on a short cable and looks out through a window in the front. It streams 1080p MJPEG at 30 fps, and the RK3576's hardware decoder unpacks the frames.
3. **Recording.** The hardware encoder saves 1080p H.265 video at about 4 Mbps in 1-minute files (about 1.8 GB per hour). The oldest files are deleted first, but only after they have been uploaded or have passed the retention limit.
4. **Detection.** About 5 frames per second go to the NPU, which runs YOLO26 converted to INT8 RKNN format. When GPS speed is under about 3 mph (parked or idling), detection is skipped. This is the "stationary-frame filtering" in CON-01, and it needs no extra hardware.
5. **Heat protection.** The unit sits in the hottest spot in the car, so the software watches the chip temperature. Above a first limit it lowers the detection rate. Above a second limit it pauses detection but keeps recording. Above a third it stops recording and shuts down cleanly. Each step is logged, so the summer heat test shows how often it happens.
6. **Geotagging.** The GPS reports position, speed and heading 10 times a second, plus a pulse-per-second (PPS) signal that keeps the system clock on GPS time within microseconds. Video and GPS share one clock, which fixes the clock-skew problem in Peer Review R2 and REQ-13. Each detection gets the vehicle's position, pushed forward along its heading by the estimated distance to the pothole (Peer Review F1).
7. **Queue.** Each detection is saved to disk as a JSON record (time, GPS, confidence, model version) plus a cropped image and a full frame. The queue survives power loss and reboots.
8. **Upload.**
   - **LTE, during the shift:** detection records and crops only (about 100 KB each), so reviewers can see them the same day.
   - **Depot Wi-Fi, after the shift:** anything left in the queue, plus a ±5 s video clip around each detection.
   - **Full video:** uploaded over Wi-Fi only, and only when time allows or the server asks for it. Never over LTE.
9. **Server.** Not part of this hardware. It stores detections, merges repeat sightings within 5 m (D1 PostGIS matching), shows them to a reviewer, checks them against existing 311 requests, and sends the approved ones to 311.
10. **Model updates.** The unit downloads new `.rknn` models over Wi-Fi and checks each one with a checksum before switching (Technical Specification I3).

### 1.3 Unit layout and power wiring

One unit is easier for city technicians to install and swap, has no long camera cable to work loose, and gives the antennas a clear view of the sky through the windshield. The cost is heat (§3), which the housing and the heat protection in §1.1 are designed around.

| Where | Parts |
|---|---|
| **Windshield unit** (about 12 × 8 × 5 cm, about 300 g, behind the mirror) | ROCK 4D + heatsink and fan (1, 2), microSD (3), RTC battery (4), camera behind a front window (5), GPS module (6) with its patch antenna on top facing the sky (7), internal Wi-Fi antenna (8), 12 V to 5 V converter (9), ignition-sense optocoupler (11), status LED and button (13), locking power connector (17), LTE modem, adapter, SIM and internal LTE antennas (18–21), all in the housing (14) on a rigid mount (15) |
| **Relay box** (at the fuse box) | Delay-off relay (10), fuse taps and fuses (12) |
| **Cable between them** (3 m, 3 conductors) | Relay-switched 12 V, ACC ignition sense (12 V, own 1 A fuse), ground. Runs along the headliner and down the A-pillar, like a dashcam hardwire kit. Plugs into the unit through a locking connector (17), so a unit can be swapped without cutting wires. |

**Why 12 V on the cable and not 5 V.** The board needs about 5 V at up to 5 A. Over 3 m of 18 AWG wire (6 m there and back), that loses about 0.6 V, and the board would brown out. At 12 V the same power is about 1.5 A, which loses about 0.2 V. The converter adds only about 1–1.5 W of heat inside the unit.

```
Fuse box                          Headliner / A-pillar             Windshield unit
+12 V constant --[5 A]--> delay relay --- 12 V switched -------> 12 V -> 5 V converter -> ROCK 4D
ACC (ignition) --[1 A]--+-> relay trigger                         
                        +-------------- ACC sense -------------> optocoupler -> GPIO
Chassis ground ------------------------ ground ----------------> GND
```

### Installation

- **Unit:** behind the rear-view mirror, inside the mirror's footprint, in the area the wipers clear. Aim it so the road from about 5 m to 25 m fills most of the frame, with the hood edge just at the bottom of the image. Record the mounting height and tilt for the forward-projection math.
- **Hood check:** with the unit mounted, put a cone in front of the vehicle and move it closer until it disappears behind the hood. Record that distance for each vehicle type.
- **Cable:** along the headliner and down the A-pillar trim, like a dashcam hardwire kit. Route it behind the side curtain airbag, never in front of it.
- **Relay box:** at the fuse box. Add-a-fuse taps go in the interior fuse box (one constant, one ACC), with ground to the chassis. Only on fleet vehicles with the city's approval. Borrowed test cars use the 12 V socket plug.

### Open items

- Confirm the ROCK 4D version with onboard Wi-Fi is in stock. (USB-C power input is confirmed: 5 V only, at least 3 A.)
- Run the YOLO26m and YOLO26s `.rknn` benchmark on the RK3576 (T10), and check INT8 accuracy on the `clean_split` test set.
- Confirm the EG25-G is approved for the carrier the city uses, and pick the IoT SIM and data plan.
- Ask about the electrical system on Community Responder vehicles (12 V vs 24 V, fuse box access, fleet rules on add-on devices).
- Find out the Community Responder vehicle model: hood height (blind zone), and whether the windshield is metal-coated (GPS and LTE through the glass).
- Check Ohio's windshield-obstruction rules and the fleet's policy for a device behind the mirror.
- Summer heat test of the full unit in a parked, sun-soaked car, early, before building more units.
